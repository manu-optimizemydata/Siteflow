Siteflow looks like an ERP for construction and interior-fit-out companies. Here’s each part in plain terms, following the sidebar from top to bottom.

Top of the sidebar

Pulse: the home dashboard. One screen showing what’s on track, what’s slipping, and what needs a decision.

My site: a view for the person working on a particular site, showing only that site’s work.

Activity: a log of recent changes (who updated what, and when).

Business (getting work in)

Leads: people who have enquired but haven’t become customers yet (e.g. “Follow up Nikhil Bansal, Indiranagar retail shell”).

Clients: customers you’ve won, with their contact details and projects.

Estimates: your internal, rough cost calculation for a job (“this will cost us about X”).

Quotations: the formal price offer you send to the client, usually based on the estimate plus your margin.

Projects (running the work)

Project portfolio: the list of all projects with client, location, status, and progress.

Schedule: the timeline of milestones and tasks (a Gantt-style plan). This is where “milestone planned end date has passed” gets detected.

Site reports: daily or weekly updates from the site (work done, workers present, photos, problems).

Tasks and issues: to-dos and problems assigned to people, with due dates. Overdue ones show on the dashboard.

Handover: the final stage where you complete snagging (fixing small defects) and formally hand the finished project to the client.

Operations (buying and supplying)

Materials: the catalogue of materials you use (cement, tiles, wiring) with rates and stock.

Material requests: the site team asking for materials (“we need 200 bags of cement”).

Purchase orders: the official order you place with a supplier after a request is approved.

Suppliers: vendors you buy materials from, with their rates and history.

Subcontractors: outside teams you hire for specific work (electrical, plumbing, carpentry), including what you owe them.

Finance

Budget and cost: planned budget versus actual spend per project.

Reports: summary reports across projects, money, and progress.

The dashboard itself

Filters at the top narrow everything below by project type, manager, status, and date.

The five summary cards:

Active projects (7): projects currently running or in handover.

At risk or delayed (4): projects behind schedule or in trouble.

Overdue tasks (5): tasks still open after their due date.

Receivables (₹1.36 Cr): money clients owe you on invoices already sent.

Cost variance (-₹12.55 Cr): actual spend minus budget. The card says a positive number means over budget, so negative means under budget. That’s a large number and probably just demo data, so treat it with caution.

Worth a look: an automatic alert, here “Villa 14 is behind plan”. It says it comes from demo rules, not a real AI model.

Project health: a one-line status for each project showing percent complete, the next milestone, and tags like Delayed, On track, At risk, Active, or Handover.

Needs attention: items waiting on a person, either overdue follow-ups or approvals (prop hire, petty cash, extra meeting room, quotation review).
